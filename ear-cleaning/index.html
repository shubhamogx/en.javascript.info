The process

Four careful steps in clinic.

Professional cleaning is measured and visual — never forced. Here’s what typically happens.

  1. 01

    Look first

    An otoscope checks the canal and eardrum so the clinician knows what’s safe to remove and what should be left alone.

  2. 02

    Soften if needed

    Cerumenolytics or warm water may loosen dense wax so it can move without scraping sensitive skin.

  3. 03

    Clear with control

    Irrigation, micro-suction, or gentle instrumentation removes blockage under direct view — stopping if you feel pain or dizziness.

  4. 04

    Recheck and advise

    A final look confirms the drum is intact, then you get simple aftercare: keep dry for a day, avoid digging, return if symptoms linger.

Safety

What not to push into the canal.

Home tools often pack wax deeper. Outer-ear hygiene is enough for most people.

  • Skip cotton swabs inside the canal — they irritate skin and shove wax inward.
  • Avoid sharp objects, keys, or hairpins; one slip can injure the eardrum.
  • Don’t use ear candles; they don’t remove wax and can burn skin.
  • Only use drops labeled for ears, and stop if they sting sharply or you have a known perforation.

Care

See a clinician when hearing dims or pain appears.

Book care sooner if you notice muffled hearing, fullness that won’t settle, drainage, fever, or sudden dizziness — especially after swimming or a cold.
