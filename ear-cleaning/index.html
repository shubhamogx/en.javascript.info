Drops beat buds. Outer wipe beats digging.

The safest everyday clean is the outer ear only. Softening drops help when wax builds up — cotton buds usually make it worse.

Safest method How to use drops

Best safe way

Clean the outside. Soften the inside. Don’t poke.

Healthy ears usually self-clean. Prefer these steps before anything that goes deep into the canal.

  1. 01

    Wipe the outer ear

    After a shower, use a soft washcloth on the visible outer ear and entrance only. That removes loose wax without pushing it inward.

  2. 02

    Use softening drops if you feel blocked

    Pharmacy wax drops, or plain mineral/olive oil if your clinician agrees, can loosen wax over a few days so it migrates out naturally.

  3. 03

    Skip cotton buds inside the canal

    Buds pack wax deeper, scrape skin, and can injure the eardrum. They are for outer folds only — if at all.

  4. 04

    See a clinician for stubborn blockage

    If hearing stays muffled, or you have pain, drainage, or dizziness, get irrigation or micro-suction done under direct view — safer than DIY digging.

Ear drops

How to use wax-softening drops safely.

Drops are usually the best home option for mild wax buildup — if your eardrum is intact and there’s no infection.

  • Warm the bottle in your hands so the liquid isn’t cold.
  • Lie on your side, pull the outer ear gently up and back, and place the labeled number of drops in the canal.
  • Stay still 5–10 minutes, then sit up and wipe only what reaches the outer opening.
  • Repeat for a few days as the product label says — don’t force rinse with pressure at home unless a clinician taught you how.
  • Stop and get checked if drops sting sharply, or if you have ear tubes, a hole in the drum, recent surgery, drainage, or severe pain.

Cotton buds / ear buds

Why buds are the riskiest “clean.”

They feel precise, but clinically they’re a common cause of packed wax and canal injury.

  • Not for the canal. Anything that fits past the outer opening tends to push cerumen toward the drum.
  • Outer ear only. If you use a bud at all, limit it to the visible rim and folds — never deep.
  • Better swaps. Soft cloth for outside; pharmacy drops for inside softening; clinic cleaning for true blockage.
  • Also avoid. Ear candles, keys, hairpins, and high-pressure home syringing without guidance.

Get help

See a clinician when hearing dims or pain appears.

Book care if muffled hearing won’t settle after drops, or you notice pain, drainage, fever, sudden dizziness, or you’ve had ear surgery / a perforated drum.
